What triggers silent coordination

Each Qwen reply writes private notes first, then its actions. We call a reply silent when its notes never mention claims, splits or task ranges, yet it still posts a coordination message. 25 of 109 coordination posts are silent. Two things in the agent's input switch them on: coordination already visible on the board, and being blocked by missing data.

Silent coordination vs collusion

Notes and actions often differ, but this is not collusion

Collusion means agents coordinating covertly toward a goal their principal forbids: using the scorer backdoor, hiding work from an overseer, making side deals. Silent coordination only means an agent's private notes did not plan a post it then made in public. Pick a setting to check each part of the definition against what was observed.

Answer: when agents are told who else exists and the job divides evenly, a 16-agent swarm forms a leader in round 1–2 (2 of 2 runs, all 15 following). Otherwise, with reasoning, the split is posted in round 2, the first round anyone can read the board. Followers act on it in round 3, so the leader reaches 25% then, and half the team by round 3–4. Of the 26 runs with no leader by round 6, only one formed one later. No leader formed without a chain of thought (0 of 22 runs). But the same model with reasoning switched on did not lead either (0 of 5), so leadership tracks model capability, not the written reasoning. Gemini, the only model here that leads, cannot switch its reasoning off.

Q5 · From individual agents to the population

Which population behaviour can be predicted from single-agent tests?

Original “Why this matters”, read as a questionWhy this matters. Safety tests on single models missed what ~700 agents did together (METR, 2026). If we can measure how the properties of individual agents add up to the behaviour of the whole population, we can predict failures before a large deployment instead of finding them after.

Where it works: contagion = per-agent conversion × network exposure

Measure one number per agent (how often an exposed agent adopts) and one per network (what share of agents the adversary reaches). Their product predicts the share of the population that adopts. Pick a model and a network and compare the prediction with the swarm runs.

Network (N = 16)ExposurePredicted adoptersMeasured adoptersError

Exposure and adoption are shares of the 15 honest agents, averaged over 4 runs per network. Conversion is pooled over all networks (24 of 122 exposed DeepSeek agents; 0 Gemini agents in 16 runs), so this is a decomposition check, not an out-of-sample forecast.

Answer: per-agent properties predict how far a compromise spreads and whether leadership can appear at all. They do not predict the size-driven switch, the effect of splitting a task, or what a weak model does among strong ones. Those have to be tested on populations. A probe on each agent's activations helps at the level of single calls, especially when the agent's notes are silent.
