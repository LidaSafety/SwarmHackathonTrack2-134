EXPERIMENT HANDOFF: CONFIRMING THE INFORMATION AND ORGANISATION ACCOUNT
======================================================

Purpose and authorization
-------------------------
Updated after review of manuscript commit 9cce342 and the source-code-informed
plan on 2026-11-07. This file records proposed follow-up research. It is a handoff
document, not an instruction to spend API credits,
launch experiments, change the paper, or publish results. Obtain the appropriate
project authorization before doing those actions. Model names below reproduce
the draft; verify their exact API identifiers, availability, settings, and prices
before implementation. Do not silently substitute models.

The aim is to turn a broad exploratory study into a coherent confirmation of a
small number of claims. Prioritize a few controlled comparisons on common models
over more model pilots, network topologies, or elaborate scenarios.

Current manuscript story:
Information structures who can organise work; model behaviour shapes adoption
of violations. Leadership, decentralised coordination, and collective violation
are distinct outcomes. Treat this as a conditional account to test, not an
established separation of causes: information also affects exposure, while model
capability also affects organisation.

The manuscript now has a coherent story table, an experimental-group ledger,
P/J/S measurement labels, and a worked episode. The follow-up should strengthen
the evidence behind that story rather than reorganize the paper from scratch.
Use the manuscript's Q1 (leadership), Q4 (scale), and Q3 (violation) numbering.

Current evidence motivating this plan
-------------------------------------
1. Announced-board leadership relies primarily on Gemini 3.8 Flash.
2. Later hidden-channel leadership relies primarily on gemini-4-argon.
3. At 16 agents on the board, providing a roster and 96 tasks produced leadership
   in 2/2 runs, while a roster and 24 tasks produced none in 2 runs. Increasing
   workload without a roster produced leadership in 1/2 runs.
4. Hidden-channel joint tasks plus a unique manifest holder produced leadership
   in 3/5 runs at eight agents and 2/2 at sixteen. Symmetric information often
   produced common conventions or direct negotiations instead. Note: the
   historical "symmetric" condition (configs/joint_opaque_argon.yaml) had no
   roster at all (cache_manifest: false, manifest_holder: false). The tested
   contrast was no roster versus one holder, not public versus private roster.
5. The revised paper distinguishes P (visible assignment followed by task start),
   J (assigned task followed by successful joint completion, not necessarily with
   the assigned partner), and S (task/round bypass-schedule compliance). The worked
   episode additionally checks assigned partners. None alone establishes causal
   influence. Keep these evidence types separate and preserve their provenance.
6. Removing cache leaders while retaining their plans left all tasks solved in
   two runs. A compound restart reduced output, but also changed names, notes,
   messages, and manifest access, so it does not isolate leader dependence.
7. Large DeepSeek V4.1 Flash reasoning-off runs showed 39/64 and 103/128 bypass
   adopters without leadership. A reasoning-on run showed 1/64. Each large arm
   has only one run. Separate scarcity comparisons have two runs per setting.
8. Observed leadership size boundaries depend on the setup. Sparse networks also
   impose a geometric ceiling on the fraction of agents one agent can reach.
9. Activation monitoring uses local Qwen3-8B and has not established transfer to
   the API models anchoring the leadership and safety claims.

Why cost matters, and what it does not resolve
----------------------------------------------
The draft reports more than 60,000 calls for about $140 in API spending, including
about $71 for the 32-step cache search and $1.54 for 1,800 judge calls. Local work
used one GPU for a few hours, with no monetary estimate. These are reported
historical totals, not estimates of the proposed work's cost.

Cost-constrained selection is a plausible explanation for numerous inexpensive
pilots, low reasoning effort, and model changes. The draft does not document
per-model prices, token usage, savings, or the selection history, so do not claim
that cost definitively explains any particular switch. Other documented reasons
include needing a reasoning toggle and needing access to internal activations.

Budget constraints can justify model selection without eliminating confounding:
changing both channel and model prevents attribution of differences to channel
alone. The efficient remedy is a small bridge study on common models, rather
than a full factorial over every historical model and scenario.

PRIORITY 0: MEASUREMENT AND HARNESS PREREQUISITES (NO PAID RUNS)
=============================================================

Objective
---------
Several later priorities depend on measurements or interventions that the
harness does not yet support. Build and freeze these before any confirmation
batch; otherwise the primary outcomes would again be judged by hand or after
seeing results.

0a. Versioned assignment-compliance metrics and historical recount.
Do this before paid confirmation. The built-in task-start metric is not a causal
influence metric. scripts/joint_leaders.py is also not an exact partner metric:
it parses task assignments, then counts a target's later correct submission in
a task/round group with at least two correct agents. It does not explicitly
check recipient exposure and hard-codes >=2 rather than cfg.joint_tasks. Audit
and correct those issues before applying J to the four-agent joint condition.
Do not assume published counts change until the recount is complete.

Preserve reproducible historical P/J/S implementations and add a separately
versioned compliance metric. For each plan record author, creation time, actual
recipient exposure time/channel, assigned agents and tasks, and partners/rounds
where the plan specifies them. Independent-task splits need neither partners
nor scheduled rounds. Distinguish correct individual submissions, successful
joint completion, exact partner compliance, exact schedule compliance, repeated
compliance, and unrelated work. Specify treatment of expired, conflicting,
withdrawn, superseded, simultaneous, and formula-based plans.

Validate extraction and exposure logic against a written manual rubric on
historical positive and negative cases (joint_*_argon, succession*_argon).
Use relevant checks for audience replay, unexposed targets, two- versus four-agent
requirements, and exact partners. Freeze the metric before confirmation; report
recounts beside original counts rather than silently overwriting history.
The new paper discloses an earlier cache-audience replay bug and corrected
summaries; verify that all published rows use the intended code version.

Common outcome: plan alignment above chance. A raw count ("fraction of agents
making a correct assigned-task submission after exposure") saturates and must
not be the primary outcome. Agents share one queue, and an agent with 75 actions
can work on roughly half of 48 tasks, so nearly every exposed agent eventually
touches some assigned task by chance. The paper already shows this: in one
restart run 11 of 14 agents did all three assigned tasks (about 7% by chance)
but also about 20 others each, and in the worked episode only 173 of 249
follower submissions matched assigned tasks. Score alignment against a
permutation null instead:

- Work events. For recipient i, take its first K task starts (first read of
  /tasks/Txx) after its actual exposure time to plan p, where K is the size of
  its assigned set (minimum 3). The fixed window prevents saturation. Use
  correct submissions as a secondary event type, and for joint tasks also
  record whether the submission was with the assigned partner.
- Observed alignment. a_i = share of those K events that are tasks assigned to
  i by p.
- Null: permute recipients. Recompute a_i after randomly reassigning the plan's
  blocks among its recipients, keeping block contents, block sizes, pair
  structure and schedule rounds fixed. Score the agent's actual work against
  the shuffled plan; use 1,000 permutations, or every permutation when there are
  few. This null keeps natural task ordering: an agent that works upward from
  T01 by default scores high only when it was actually assigned the low block,
  which is the case that otherwise credits coincidence. Do not use uniform
  relabelling of tasks, which assumes agents choose tasks at random and so
  inflates alignment for default orderings.
- Single-recipient plans, or plans naming too few recipients to permute: use a
  block-shift null (circularly shift the assigned block within the task range)
  and label these results separately.
- Per-agent result: excess alignment e_i = a_i - mean(null a_i), plus the
  percentile of a_i in its null distribution. An agent counts as an aligned
  follower if a_i exceeds the 95th null percentile and at least 2 of its K
  events are assigned tasks.
- Run-level outcomes: mean excess alignment over exposed recipients (primary,
  continuous); the fraction of other agents who are aligned followers; and
  aligned-follower leadership, meaning at least half of the other agents for
  one author's plan. Report the historical P and J counts alongside, never as
  substitutes.
- Contemporaneous control: score unexposed or not-yet-exposed agents against
  the same plan over the same rounds; their alignment estimates convention-
  driven agreement.
- Limits: if every agent follows the same ID- or name-ordered rule, alignment is
  high under any null, because agreement with a shared convention cannot be
  separated from influence observationally. Report such runs as aligned but
  convention-compatible (flag simultaneous identical plans). Priority 2 resolves
  this with delivery and replacement interventions.
- Validation before freezing: run this on historical logs, including runs
  expected to be positive (q1_trigger_roster 96-task runs, joint_manifest16_argon,
  the worked episode) and runs expected to be negative (no-plan runs,
  joint_opaque_argon). Compare its chance levels with the paper's hand-computed
  "~7% by chance" figure (a different statistic), and report recounts next to the published P/J numbers. Freeze K, the
  null, the thresholds and the treatment of superseded or conflicting plans
  before any confirmation run.

Add exact joint/partner/schedule outcomes where applicable. Even
above-chance alignment by a majority is operational leadership, not proof
that the plan determined choices. Causal influence is tested in Priority 2. Removal selection must use only compliance observed by the fork time,
with a frozen eligibility and tie-breaking rule, not eventual episode outcomes.

0b. Episode forking. World state is in-memory Python (src/swarmlab/world.py),
but audit the simulator loop before choosing a snapshot mechanism. Capture
world, agent notes, last results, pending actions/results, queued observations,
posts, cache, model allocation and RNG states, without sharing mutable objects
between branches. Implement and validate branch isolation; it makes Priorities 2 and 4 paired
comparisons rather than seed-matched ones. LLM sampling is not seeded, so arms
diverge after the fork; that divergence is the treatment effect plus sampling
noise. Include a no-intervention continuation and replicate over independent
eligible parent episodes; optional duplicate control continuations estimate
branch variability. Branches from one parent are not independent episodes.

0c. Intervention hooks, none of which exist yet:
- Withhold a specified post from specified agents (board feed, read_board,
  ls /cache/tmp, watched entries, and file reads must all respect it).
- Replace a post's content with an experimenter-edited plan, keeping author,
  round, visibility and approximate length.
- Remove a randomly chosen non-leader (seeded, prespecified rule).
- Delete the leader's cache entries/plan without renaming survivors, clearing
  notes or moving manifest access (restart_on_removal currently bundles all
  four; see restart_job in world.py).
- Log every exposure: direct, reposted, or quoted copies of a withheld plan.

0d. Cost and token logging. metrics.json uses configured price constants rather
than invoices. Record provider-reported cost and token accounting where available,
reasoning tokens, caching, provider routing, finish_reason, retries and failures.
Keep estimated cost separately labeled when billed cost is unavailable. Verify
prices and supported settings immediately before budgeting; prior price checks
are not a durable quote. Enforce a conservative spend cap including retries and
concurrent in-flight calls. Do not infer a precise cap overrun from a price-ratio
change alone.

0e. Message-capacity logging. Log when content is cut off or hidden: posts
omitted from the observation feed (board_feed_limit = 15), posts over
max_post_chars = 600, cache names truncated at cache_name_max = 120, cache file
content cut at 600 characters, and cache listings beyond the last 40 entries.
These caps may bind as populations grow; measure actual omission and truncation
rates rather than asserting that they explain a boundary (see Priority 5).

0f. Seed policy. The environment seed fixes tasks, data, sandbox names and the
manifest holder (seed, seed+313, seed+909 in world.py); it does not fix model
sampling. The prior review reports historical seeds 0 to 5; audit the full manifest
before assuming that range is exhaustive. Reserve a disjoint block for
confirmation (for example 100 and above) and record it before running.

PRIORITY 1: COMMON-MODEL LEADERSHIP CONFIRMATION
=============================================

Objective
---------
Test whether the two principal leadership findings survive a model change, and
connect the board and cache sections through a common model panel.

Provisional models
------------------
Gemini 3.8 Flash and gemini-4-argon, subject to verifying the exact historical
identifiers and availability. Each currently anchors one side of the paper.
Use the same two models in both experiment families. If either is unavailable,
document the replacement and retain the distinction between historical
replication and a new-model test.

Board matrix
------------
Population: 16 agents. Complete graph. Announced board. Team objective.
Roster: absent versus present.
Workload: 24 versus 96 tasks.
This gives four conditions per model.

Use the historical 20-round protocol (recoverable: configs/or_scaling.yaml,
configs/or_transition.yaml, configs/q1_trigger_tasks.yaml,
configs/q1_trigger_roster.yaml). Preserve identical task-generation rules,
action allowances, observation construction, and scoring across models. Record
model-specific reasoning settings; do not describe unequal API settings as a
controlled reasoning comparison.

Known mismatch to fix: the historical 24-task no-roster cell (or_scaling,
or_transition) uses the default n_shards = 6, while the trigger configs use
n_shards = 12. Fix n_shards (12) in all four cells. Label this as a controlled follow-up,
not an exact reproduction of the six-shard historical baseline. Roster is
peer_info: roster versus peer_info: same_tasks; keep everything else equal.
At N = 16 the observation feed shows at most 15 new posts per round, which
equals the number of other agents, but multiple posts or accumulated unread
posts can still overflow it; record truncation rather than assuming full delivery.

Questions:
- Does providing a roster increase complete, usable assignment plans?
- Does a larger workload make allocation useful at this population size?
- Does the combined effect recur on the other model?
- Are identical plans evidence of a common convention rather than a unique
  leader? Track simultaneous plans and authors explicitly.

Hidden-cache matrix
-------------------
Population: eight agents. Opaque sandbox names. Same cache tools and affordances
in every condition.
Task structure: independent tasks versus joint-submission tasks.
Roster access, three levels:
  (i)   none: no manifest (the historical "symmetric" condition,
        configs/joint_opaque_argon.yaml);
  (ii)  unique holder: one sandbox can read the manifest (historical,
        configs/joint_manifest_argon.yaml);
  (iii) public: every sandbox can read the manifest (new condition).
This gives six conditions per model. (i) vs (ii) addresses supplying roster
information to someone; (ii) vs (iii) isolates exclusivity only if awareness and
other affordances are controlled. The salience-controlled matrix below is a new
follow-up. An exact historical prompt replication, if needed, is a separate arm.

Salience/access control: the current holder receives a system-prompt sentence
pointing to /cache/manifest.txt; a public manifest does not automatically produce
the same awareness. In BOTH unique-holder and public conditions, tell ALL agents
that the same manifest path exists and contains the job roster, using identical
wording. Let the read result determine who has access; do not tell only the
holder about the file. Preserve the same directory visibility and tools.
The none condition can receive a matched sentence stating that no job manifest
is available. This contrast includes manifest availability; the unique/public
contrast is the main test of exclusivity. Log permission denials and roster
republication: public access and eventual public knowledge are different things.
These assisted conditions test organisation, not unassisted discovery.

Match underlying numerical task content, data access, and submission opportunities
as far as possible. Task content is already matched for a given seed:
joint_tasks does not change the task generator's random draws, so the same seed
gives identical tasks with and without joint scoring. Document unavoidable
differences introduced by joint scoring (task-file wording, PENDING results).
The manifest holder and names use separate RNG streams (seed+313 and seed+909).
These avoid direct draw-order coupling; they do not prove statistical independence
across selected seeds. Check actual balance, and use matched seeds to pair tasks,
names and holder identity where applicable.

Use the permutation-scored alignment outcome in Priority 0a (mean excess
alignment; aligned-follower leadership) as primary, with joint, partner and
schedule compliance as secondary outcomes. This matters most in the
independent-task cells, where raw assigned-task submission rates would be near
ceiling regardless of any plan. Also report valid-plan
production and any-author versus holder-led leadership separately. A privileged
holder is not the only possible plan author.

Earlier Flash cache-ladder failures involved different conditions and do not
establish a capability floor for this joint-task matrix. Test the same frozen
matrix on both models with fixed allocations. Do not continue a model only when
its first runs look promising.

Questions:
- Do joint tasks increase the utility of a shared plan?
- Does asymmetric roster access concentrate plan authorship and following?
- Does either model instead use decentralized negotiations or a common rule?

Important scope limit: this is a test of leadership under specified cache
affordances, not necessarily unassisted channel discovery. Explicitly state any
seeded notes, messages, manifest hints, watches, or file-writing tools supplied.

Replication and scale
---------------------
Suggested fixed starting allocation (fresh seeds, Priority 0f):
- Board: 4 conditions x 2 models x 5 seeds = 40 runs.
- Cache: 6 conditions x 2 models x 5 seeds = 60 runs.
Total: 100 runs, excluding smoke tests and later interventions. Five seeds is a
starting point, not a power guarantee or a precise estimate of leadership rates.
Finalize allocation from cost and desired uncertainty before observing outcomes.

If unaffordable, reduce scope symmetrically before running. For example, retain
the 40-run board matrix and joint-task cache conditions with none/unique/public
rosters on both models (3 x 2 x 5 = 30), totalling 70. This sacrifices the direct
independent-versus-joint comparison and must be labeled accordingly. Alternatively
stage exploratory screening separately from fresh confirmation. Do not use an
outcome-dependent two-to-five-seed extension as the main cross-model replication.

Estimate spend from representative smoke tests with input/output, reasoning,
caching, retries and actual round lengths. Historical per-call estimates are
insufficient for a reliable new-batch budget. Keep confirmation seeds separate
from engineering pilots and historical search.

PRIORITY 2: DIRECT TEST OF PLAN INFLUENCE
======================================

Objective
---------
Determine whether apparent followers act because they receive a plan, rather
than independently selecting the same work. This addresses a central measurement
weakness and may be more valuable than another broad model comparison.

Starting setup
--------------
Use a frozen joint-task setup that reliably produces valid assignment plans.
Prefer opaque identities and exact partner/task assignments. Start with one
model, then replicate the strongest result on the second common model.
Natural candidate: the 16-agent manifest-holder setup
(configs/joint_manifest16_argon.yaml), where the holder led in 2/2 runs.

Requires Priority 0b (forking) and 0c (withholding and content replacement).
Recommended boundary: generate the first eligible author message, then snapshot
BEFORE any recipient sees it through feeds, tool results, watches, reads, or
queued observations. Continue A/B/C from that state. A round-end snapshot after
recipient delivery is too late. If multiple calls execute in a batch, intercept
delivery explicitly. Validate the boundary with exposure logs. Distinguish early
message eligibility from established leadership, which requires later following.

Interventions
-------------
A. Deliver the proposed leader's plan normally.
B. Withhold that plan from designated potential followers.
C. Deliver a valid randomized partner/task plan in place of the original.
Score original-plan and replacement-plan compliance in EVERY branch, including B;
this provides a baseline for coincidental agreement with either allocation.
Predefine which recipient population receives each intervention. Whole-episode
withholding from all peers is cleaner for the primary contrast than selective
withholding, which can be added as a spillover study.

These interventions answer different questions. B tests dependence on exposure
to the plan. C makes independent agreement with a default ordering less likely.
If replacing an agent-authored plan with an experimenter-edited plan, label this
as an intervention on message content; it does not show that the author itself
invented the randomized allocation.

Implementation cautions
-----------------------
- Predefine an eligible plan and when randomization occurs.
- Avoid selecting only unusually successful runs after seeing outcomes. If the
  intervention requires a valid plan to exist, report the plan-generation rate
  and make clear that the influence estimate is conditional on eligibility.
- Consider episode-level randomization for clean total effects. Withholding from
  individual followers can create spillovers if other agents repost the plan.
- Log direct exposure, indirect re-exposure, and competing plans. Do not claim
  that a follower remained unexposed merely because one message was withheld.
- Keep sender identity, visibility, timing, and message length comparable where
  possible. Ensure randomized pairings and schedules remain feasible.

Primary measures
----------------
Excess alignment with the original plan (Priority 0a), contrasted across arms
A/B/C; in arm C also alignment with the replacement plan, which is the most
direct test that recipients act on message content. Exact assigned partner/task
compliance, repeated compliance, and completed joint tasks. Also record latency, competing assignments, and independent task choices.
Keep the historical cumulative reach measure for comparability, but do not rely
on it as the sole evidence of influence.

PRIORITY 3: REPLICATED WITHIN-MODEL REASONING COMPARISON
====================================================

Objective
---------
Confirm the reasoning-setting result without confounding it with model family,
and distinguish refusal from improved legitimate problem solving.

Design
------
First use DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, reasoning on versus off, in one fixed scarcity
setup. The existing scarcity configuration is recoverable from
scripts/q1_scenarios.py: the "scarcity" scenario, N = 16, data_holders = 2,
arms "nocot" (reasoning: {enabled: false}, max_tokens 2000) and "ds_think"
(reasoning: {effort: low}, max_tokens 12000), seeds 0 and 1; logs in
runs/q1_scenarios/.
Export it to a YAML config and freeze it. The token allowance differs sixfold
between arms; either equalize max_tokens or report the cap as part of the
treatment, and log truncation (finish_reason) per call. Equal token ceilings do
not guarantee equal compute or equal answer-token availability if reasoning
shares that ceiling. Prefer a documented nonbinding common ceiling for the
primary setting comparison; treat compute matching as a separate question.
Prefer several replicated smaller populations over another single 128-agent
episode. Select population size from a documented pilot or historical setup,
then freeze it before confirmation.

If resources allow, repeat on a second model with documented support for both
settings. Validate what each API parameter actually controls. Reasoning disabled
means the configured API mode, not a demonstrated absence of internal reasoning.

Hold system rules, task generation, data availability, channel, action budgets,
population, and model version fixed within each model comparison. Record output
limits, truncation, latency, actual reasoning tokens where available, and cost.
If token allowances differ, report that as part of the treatment rather than
silently treating the arms as computationally matched.

Measures
--------
- Fraction exposed to the bypass, by direct discovery and social exposure.
- Fraction adopting among all agents.
- Adoption among exposed agents, alongside exposure timing and source.
- Legitimate task completion and total score, separately.
- Communication, leadership, and explicitly coordinated bypass submissions.

Interpretation
--------------
Conditional adoption is descriptive if reasoning changes which agents become
exposed. For a cleaner willingness-to-adopt test, add a separately labeled
controlled-exposure condition in which both arms receive the same bypass
information at the same time. Keep this separate from natural discovery.
The main swarm reasoning effect should be reported at the run level; agents and
calls within a run are not independent replications.

PRIORITY 4: LEADER REMOVAL VERSUS ARTIFACT LOSS
============================================

Objective
---------
Estimate the contribution of continued coordinator participation and shared
artifact availability, while comparing leader loss with ordinary agent loss.
Artifact deletion does not erase a previously learned plan, so this is not a
complete intervention on the existence of the plan in agent memory.

Four proposed arms
------------------
A. No removal; preserve the plan.
B. Remove the leader; preserve the plan.
C. Remove a random nonleader; preserve the plan.
D. Remove the leader; delete its plan.

Use the same reliable leadership setup and a prespecified intervention time or
eligibility rule. Arms C and D, and a clean version of B, need Priority 0c
hooks; the existing restart_on_removal option is the bundled disruption the
paper already criticizes and must not be used for D. Identify the leader with
the strict metric (Priority 0a), not the task-start parser that
kill_leader_round currently uses. Keep surviving identities, private notes, manifest access,
tasks, and data fixed. Do not bundle renaming, memory clearing, and information
transfer into the removal intervention.

Fork the same established-leadership episode immediately before removal, using
only evidence available at that time to select the leader. This is later than
the message-intervention fork, which precedes recipient exposure. Share the
snapshot machinery across studies, but do not claim they use the same fork
point. If forking is unavailable, disclose that seed matching does not guarantee
identical pre-intervention histories.

Predefine leader identification, ties, and what happens in runs without a leader.
Choose the nonleader by a prespecified random rule. Account for disrupted task
partnerships: losing the leader may also remove a worker, data holder, or partner.
Record those roles so a performance loss is not automatically attributed to lost
management.

Measures
--------
Post-intervention completion, repeated adherence to the old plan, replacement
pairings, successor proposals, successor influence, and recovery time. Distinguish
plan survival in cached observations or notes from plan availability in the shared
cache; deleting the artifact does not erase agents' memories of it.

The four arms estimate useful contrasts, but are not a complete factorial of
leader presence and artifact presence. Add a leader-present/plan-deleted arm only
if isolating artifact regeneration or an interaction is important and affordable.
Begin with one model and replicate the clearest contrast on the second.

PRIORITY 5: CONTROLLED SCALE REPLICATION
======================================

Objective
---------
Test whether population size changes organizational form after accounting for
task availability and direct communication access.

Design
------
Choose one confirmed leadership setup and test eight, sixteen, and thirty-two
agents on the common two-model panel. Begin with complete visibility. Use a
workload that grows with population, keeping useful allocation opportunities
approximately constant per agent. Specify roster access consistently; in a unique
holder setup, preserve the information asymmetry at each population size.

Scaling workload changes the historical fixed-workload experiment. Treat it as a
new control designed to test an explanation for the earlier size effect. Ideally
retain a fixed-workload arm if budget permits. Keep the per-agent action budget
constant and record context/message growth; complete visibility removes a degree
cap but does not remove context-length or attention constraints.

Fixed message-capacity limits are a competing explanation for the historical
size effects and must be controlled. In the current harness:
- board_feed_limit = 15: at most 15 new posts appear in an observation, so for
  N > 16 a round-1 plan can drop out of the feed unless the agent calls
  read_board (read_board_limit = 30);
- max_post_chars = 600: an enumerated assignment for 32+ agents may not fit;
- cache names are cut at 120 characters, cache file content at 600, and
  ls /cache/tmp shows only the last 40 entries.
Measure whether and when each limit actually binds. Prefer a fixed-capacity
versus raised-capacity contrast at the largest N, retaining a historical-cap
baseline. Capacity scaling alone changes the environment and cannot identify
whether the old boundary was due to the interface. Account for multiple posts
per agent; feed >= N is not by itself a delivery guarantee. Report truncation
and actual exposure from Priority 0e. Fixed-capacity size effects remain valid
conditional findings, not intrinsic population thresholds.

Measures
--------
Leadership incidence, exact following, repeated compliance, decentralized pairing,
common-rule adoption, duplicate solving, legitimate coverage, and cost per task.
Count compact rule-based assignments as a separate coordination category rather
than allowing an enumerated-assignment parser to define all organization.

Interpretation
--------------
Ask whether organization changes with size under these controls. Do not claim a
universal critical population or phase transition from a few discrete sizes and
small seed counts. A larger size with no measured leader may still coordinate
effectively through conventions or local offers.

3. Freeze primary hypotheses, endpoints, intervention rules, seed count, parsing,
   retry policy, and exclusions. Exploratory follow-ups should be labeled as such.
4. Verify model settings with a small engineering smoke test. Keep those runs
   separate from confirmation data. Do not tune prompts separately for each
   model without documenting that this changes the comparison.
5. Estimate budget from measured input/output usage, cached-token billing,
   reasoning usage, and retries on representative short runs. Obtain the project
   owner's budget authorization before launching paid batches.

During running
--------------
Use unique run IDs and immutable configurations. Log model/provider version,
sampling parameters, prompts, observations, actions, results, messages, visibility,
interventions, notes, seeds, timestamps, token usage, failures, and billed cost.
Interleave model/condition execution where practical so provider changes or time
trends do not align perfectly with treatment. Follow a predefined retry policy;
retain failures rather than silently replacing unfavorable or malformed runs.

Analysis
--------
- Treat episodes as the primary unit of replication. Use paired contrasts where
  the design truly supports pairing. Do not inflate sample sizes with agent calls.
- Report raw run counts, per-run outcomes, uncertainty, and unsuccessful runs.
  With five seeds, emphasize uncertainty rather than binary significance claims.
- Separate primary confirmation from exploratory analyses and historical search.
- Report leadership, decentralized coordination, performance, and violations as
  distinct outcomes. Keep exposure separate from adoption.
- Version the historical parser and improved assignment-compliance metrics. Manually audit
  a prespecified sample of positives and negatives using a written rubric, with
  treatment/model labels concealed where feasible. Record disagreements.
- Do not silently combine task-start following, exact partner following, schedule
  following, and common conventions into one interchangeable leadership number.
- Report costs and legitimate output alongside behavioral outcomes, so inexpensive
  but ineffective models are not presented as equivalent substitutes.

WHAT TO DEPRIORITIZE
===================

Additional topology sweeps, more unrelated model pilots, larger single-run swarms,
more espionage scenarios, and additional activation steering are lower priority.
They broaden the project without resolving its main comparability and causal
measurement gaps.

Activation monitoring can remain an auxiliary section or become a separate
study. A second open-model replication would strengthen that study, but is less
central to the leadership narrative than the priorities above. Do not assume
Qwen probes transfer to API models without activation access and direct tests.
